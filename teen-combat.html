How Teen Combat Runs

Not a Kids Class.
Not a Watered-Down
Adult One.

Physical

Real Technique, Progressive Contact

Technique is taught to a standard before intensity is introduced. Partner work and light-contact sparring are added progressively, closely supervised, and matched to what each teenager has actually demonstrated — not their age or how long they've trained.

Mental

Trained Under Pressure, Not Just Told About It

Confidence isn't handed out for showing up. It's built by being tested under a real standard, in a safe environment, and finding out what you're actually capable of — often for the first time.

Identity

Who They Become, Not What They Learn

The technique is the vehicle. What a teenager walks out with — the way they carry themselves, what they now expect of themselves — is the actual outcome we're building toward.

Two Paths Through Eagle DS

What Happens When
a Teenager Stays.

The Youth Academy Path

One teenager came through the Youth Academy having never trained anything competitive before. Progress was slow at first — it always is — but the standard didn't move for them, and neither did the expectation that they keep showing up. What changed wasn't just technical ability. It was how they carried themselves outside the gym — at school, at home, in the way they spoke about what they were capable of. That's the outcome the Academy is actually built to produce.

The Combat Games Series Path

Another teenager took the competitive route through the Combat Games Series — training toward a real bout, under a real standard, against a real opponent. That teenager went on to place first nationally. The medal wasn't the point. What it proved was already true before the final bell: they had been tested honestly, and they had met it.

Before You Book

Questions Every Parent
Actually Asks.

Does my teenager need any experience?

No. Most teenagers start with none. The syllabus is structured so a complete beginner and someone who's trained before are both working at the right level for them from day one.

Is it safe? Will my teenager get hit?

Technique comes before intensity, every time. Sparring is introduced progressively and only once a teenager has shown the control and technical ability required — it's closely supervised throughout. Eagle DS holds a zero-concussion record across 900+ matches. The goal is skill development, not hurting a training partner.

My teenager isn't very confident. Will they fit in?

This is exactly who the programme is built for. A lot of teenagers arrive shy or unsure of themselves. The standard stays the same for everyone — that consistency is what builds the confidence, not a softer version of the training.

Does my teenager have to compete?

No. Competing through the Combat Games Series is one path, not a requirement. Plenty of teenagers train for fitness, confidence and skill and never step into a competitive bout — both paths are treated with the same seriousness.

What do you actually expect from a teenager who joins?

Not perfection. What's expected every session:

  • Effort
  • Respect
  • Accountability
  • Coachability
What should my teenager bring to their first session?

Comfortable sports clothing and a drink bottle. Equipment for the first session is provided — if they continue, you'll be advised exactly what's needed as they progress.

My teenager already plays another sport. Will this help or get in the way?

It almost always helps. Coordination, balance, agility, resilience and mental toughness built here transfer directly into whatever else they play.
